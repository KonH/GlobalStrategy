# Tech retro — 01_world-domination milestone

## Agenda
we need to understand what goes well, what goes bad, how to improve codebase and maintainability, what areas of code needs adjustments, which providers/models provide better results and how to improve productivity

## Constraints
- Meeting is capped to **1 hour**.
- Participants must prepare **before** the meeting starts: gather their own
  context and notes in advance, keep those notes to themselves, and **do not
  look at other participants' notes or draft input** before/during the
  meeting — the goal is independent, clear-sighted takes rather than
  converged/anchored ones.
- For each theme, participants should work toward **consensus** — surface
  disagreements explicitly and try to resolve or narrow them, rather than
  just stacking parallel opinions and moving on.
- The **meeting owner is responsible for timekeeping**: hold each theme to
  its budget (see time-boxed checklist below), call time when a theme is
  running long, and steer the group to close it out (consensus or explicit
  disagreement noted) so every theme gets covered before the 1-hour cap.

## Definition of done
- [ ] Each participant has stated, in their own words and from their own
      prepared notes, what went well and what went badly during the
      01_world-domination milestone.
- [ ] Concrete codebase/maintainability pain points are named with enough
      specificity to act on (not just "tech debt is bad").
- [ ] Specific areas of code are flagged as needing adjustment, with a rough
      reason why.
- [ ] Provider/model performance is compared (which providers/models gave
      better results, for what kind of task) based on participants' actual
      working experience this milestone.
- [ ] Concrete productivity-improvement ideas are captured (workflow, tooling,
      prompts, skills, process).
- [ ] `summary.md` is written by the meeting owner capturing the above as
      actionable takeaways.

## Checklist
Time budget below is for the owner to enforce; adjust live if a theme
reaches consensus early, but do not let overruns eat later themes.

- [ ] Each participant prepares independently before the meeting: review own
      session history / commits / friction points from the milestone.
      (prep happens before the clock starts)
- [ ] (~8 min) Opening round: what went well — reach consensus on the
      standout wins.
- [ ] (~8 min) What went badly / friction points — reach consensus on the
      real pain points vs. one-off complaints.
- [ ] (~10 min) Codebase & maintainability: what needs to change, where —
      converge on priority.
- [ ] (~10 min) Specific code areas needing adjustment (call out
      files/systems, not just vague categories) — converge on the short list.
- [ ] (~10 min) Provider/model comparison: which gave better results, on
      what kind of task, and why — converge on a shared take, note any
      genuine disagreement explicitly.
- [ ] (~8 min) Productivity: what would make the next milestone
      faster/smoother — converge on top ideas.
- [ ] (~6 min) Close: confirm the agreed (or explicitly disagreed) takeaways
      per theme, owner writes `summary.md`.
